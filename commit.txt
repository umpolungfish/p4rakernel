ProofLift: a proof is a control-flow graph, so Vox can audit it

Vox already enforces the close condition on x86, EVM, WASM and CPython bytecode:
mu after delta = id over a TRANSFORMED object — split, work, fuse. A Lean proof
term is a DAG under exactly that condition. A case analysis is the fork, the
branches landing on one goal is the fuse, and a sorry is a fork whose arm never
comes back. So the audit transfers with no new theory, and nearly no new code:
vox already exposes `verdict <glyph-word>`, so the lift is all that was missing.

The twelve read on a term as: recursor or match opens the fork, its branches are
the arms, the rejoin is the fuse; application is composition; a bound variable is
identity; a let is irreversible fixation; Eq.mpr and Eq.symm are the forward and
reverse morphisms. sorryAx emits an opening with nothing to pair it.

Measured, not asserted:

    p -> p by h                  ⊢⊙⊣          N   never forked
    case split, both discharged  ⊢∈⊤...∋⊣     T   split, work, fuse
    one arm sorry'd              ⊢∈...∈...∋⊣  B   a fork held open
    nothing but sorry            ⊢∈⊣          B   the claim, never rejoined

    Nat.succ_ne_zero    234 glyphs   T
    Nat.le_of_lt_succ     7 glyphs   N
    List.length_append 1020 glyphs   T
    Nat.strongRecOn      23 glyphs   N

What this says that `lake build` does not: sorry-free is a bool, and the verdict
is graded. N is the one worth having — a proof that closes without ever forking
weighed no alternatives, and for some statements that means the thing proved is
not the thing intended. Lean has no notion of that; the close condition does.

`sorry` and "unfused fork" turn out not to be an analogy. They are the same
object, and the kernel that judges machine code catches it unchanged.
