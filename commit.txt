Axiom D demoted: it proved False, and the concrete cases need no axiom

Axiom D read

  axiom holographic_closure_forces_frobenius (d) (t) (p) (pol) :
    d = if' → t = are → p ≥ ah → pol = or'

with pol universally quantified and untouched by the hypotheses. I meant it to
say that a holographically closed system carries or'. It said that EVERY
polarity is or'. Instantiate at pol := yew, discharge the three hypotheses by
rfl and le_refl, and it hands back yew = or'; Polarity.noConfusion turns that
into False. I proved False from it and printed the dependency to be sure: the
standard three plus that axiom, nothing else. So it proved anything at all, and
every theorem resting on it said nothing.

Hodge_KernelCrossing carried the inconsistency as a stated lemma —
hodge_polarity_forced_pm_sym (pol : Polarity) : pol = or' — and the chain
Axiom D → that lemma → hodge_pm_semantic_bridge → HodgeConjecture ran through
it.

It is now HolographicClosure, a predicate on an imscription that reads the
polarity off the structure instead of quantifying over it. Every field of a
concrete imscription is a constructor, so the predicate is decidable, and the
three theorems that used to invoke the axiom are now closed by `decide`:
bsd_axiom_D_forces_frobenius depends on propext alone,
bsd_frobenius_identity_is_structural and hodge_corrected_polarity_forced on no
axiom at all. The axiom was never needed for a concrete case — and using it made
those theorems vacuous, because it proved the conclusion whatever the polarity
actually was.

AxiomD_Demotion.lean is the guard. It prints the dependencies, shows the
constraint HOLDS for bsdResolved, and shows it FAILS for the same imscription
with only its polarity changed — the signature intact, so the hypotheses still
fire. That falsifiability is the whole point: it is now a claim that can be
wrong, which the axiom form could not express.

The predicate lives in Primitives/Imscription.lean because that is where
Imscription is defined; Core.lean keeps a note saying where it went and why.
