Five Erdős files given real content, and one verdict retracted

BinomialGCD: the prime-power branch via Nat.Prime.dvd_choose_pow, the digit
obstruction via one step of Lucas, and a refutation of the file's own smooth-n
mechanism — it said p ∣ n is blocked by i = p-1, but that index forces a carry,
so p ∣ C(n,p-1) always.

UnitDistance: the Lenz construction at d=4 in EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 4), two
circles of radius 1/√2 in orthogonal planes at constant distance 1, assembled to
f₄(n) ≥ ⌊n²/4⌋. Circle points are carried by unit vectors of ℝ², not angles, so
distinctness is read off coordinates.

SumsetAvoidingAP: every ✓ now names a theorem. Progression-freeness of the
sumset FORCES distinct subset sums — a repeated sum ∑B = ∑C builds the
progression ∑(B∩C), ∑B, ∑(B∪C) — which is what licenses the 2ⁿ ≤ nN+1 lower
bound that had sat in the list with no argument. g₃(5) = 60 is proved by a
pruned DFS whose soundness is itself proved: search_true shows the walk reaches
any valid configuration, so a false verdict is a theorem about every subset, not
only the visited ones.

ErdosTuranAP: two conflations fixed. Branch 1 called Σ1/aₙ = ∞ equivalent to
positive density while Branch 3 said the primes refute exactly that, and the
Belnap note read "Both True (Szemerédi proved it)" — Szemerédi is about positive
density, a strictly stronger hypothesis. k=3 for positive density is now
machine-checked from Mathlib's Roth and Behrend.

PomeranceSDR: I had recorded the q5 rerun as a kernel factual error confusing
the Jacobsthal function. Against the literature that is wrong — the two runs
answered about different quantities, f(n,n) versus max_m f(n,m), and van Doorn
2026 proves the rerun's order. The divergence is an ambiguity;
kernel_factual_errors is computed and zero.
