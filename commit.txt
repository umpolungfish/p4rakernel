Schütte tournaments: f(2)=7 closed on both sides, and a B that was not a conflict

f(2) = 7 is now machine-checked in both directions. The upper half is the Paley
tournament on ℤ/7 — i beats j when j−i is a quadratic residue, and paley7_has_S2
checks every pair. The lower half, no_S2_on_six, is an exhaustive search over
ALL 2^15 tournaments on six vertices. That search is only feasible because a
tournament is stored as the fifteen bits of its upper triangle, so antisymmetry
is a property of the encoding rather than a hypothesis — beats6_antisymm proves
that outright, and the enumeration hits each tournament exactly once. f(1) = 3
goes the same way, cyc3_has_S1 against no_S1_on_two.

The lower bound recorded in the file was the wrong one. It said f(n) ≥ 2ⁿ⁺¹−1,
proved by "double counting of dominator sets, one per vertex, each of size at
most 2ⁿ" — an argument that does not go through as written, since a vertex's
dominated set carries no such bound. The bound the literature carries is
Szekeres–Szekeres, (n+2)·2ⁿ⁻¹−1, and it is tight at both exactly known values:
4·2−1 = 7 = f(2) and 5·4−1 = 19 = f(3), where 2ⁿ⁺¹−1 gives 7 and then 15 and
misses f(3) by four. stated_bound_weaker proves the old bound is strictly weaker
for every n ≥ 3, which is also why the file's "gap between 31 and ~140" at n=4
started in the wrong place: the lower end is 47.

And the B was carried for the wrong reason. The note fused "the structural
branch claims f(n)≈2ⁿ⁺¹" with "the probabilistic branch gives f(n)≈n²·2ⁿ" into a
held dialetheia. Those are a lower bound and an upper bound; they do not
contradict each other. sandwich_nonempty proves the interval is non-empty for
every n and szekeres_le_known puts every known value inside it. A gap between
bounds is a gap, not a conflict. B stands, but on the obstructional branch —
f(n) is open for n ≥ 4 — and the branch verdicts go from T/T/T, which
contradicted the B above them, to T/T/B.

Two smaller conflations: the upper bound was headed Θ(n²·2ⁿ) over a union-bound
computation that yields only O, and Θ would have settled the file's own last
open item; and schutte_tournament_frobenius_closure was `: True`, now replaced
by f2_eq_seven, which names the two searches that actually close the value.
