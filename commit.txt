Erdős 3 at k=3, from a Roth bound and nothing else

The analytic half is proved, so the chain from a quantitative Roth bound to the
conclusion is now complete inside this file, with no axiom and no sorry anywhere
in it.

summable_blocks_of_log_bound: given r₃(N) ≤ C·N/(log N)^(1+ε) past some N₀, the
dyadic block terms r₃(2^j)/2^j sum. Substituting N = 2^j turns the bound into
K·j^-(1+ε) with K = C/(log 2)^(1+ε), and a p-series with p > 1 converges. The
exponent carries the whole argument: at ε = 0 the comparison series is harmonic
and the proof gives nothing, which is exactly why every Roth bound before
Bloom–Sisask left this problem open.

erdos_problem_3_k3_of_roth_bound: the statement resting on that hypothesis alone.
Divergent reciprocals give a three-term progression, given a Roth bound past
exponent one.

I stated it about Mathlib's rothNumberNat, and the reason matters. The corpus
already had kelley_meka_upper_bound in Imscribing.Classical.Szemeredi, and I went
to use it — but r_k there is declared `opaque r_k (k N : ℕ) : ℕ`, a constant with
no body and no connection to rothNumberNat. A bound on an opaque symbol is a
bound on an arbitrary function; it cannot discharge anything, and wiring it to
the real Roth number would take a fresh assumption of its own. So the hypothesis
here names the object Mathlib defines.

Everything in the file rests on propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound alone.
The lift reads T on three_ap_of_dense, three_ap_of_not_threeAPFree, block_sum_le,
summable_of_blocks and erdos_problem_3_k3.
