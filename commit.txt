Polynom asymptotics: the extremal configuration exists at every n and cannot be held

Taking the n-th roots of unity for the points makes ∏(z − z_i) equal to z^n − 1,
whose maximum on the circle is exactly 2 — no growth in n at all.
abs_pow_sub_one_le_two and abs_pow_sub_one_eq_two give both halves. That does not
refute Q1, and the reason is the whole difficulty of the problem: Q1 is about ONE
infinite sequence and the first n of its terms, while the n-th roots of unity and
the (n+1)-th meet only at 1, since gcd(n, n+1) = 1. The extremal configuration
exists at every n and no single sequence can hold two consecutive ones.

Q3 asks for a power saving rather than growth. M_k ≥ 1 for every configuration,
because ∏(z − z_i) is monic of degree k, so Σ_{k≤n} M_k ≥ n comes for free and
Q3's n^{1+c} lives entirely in the exponent. Q2 gives M_k > k^c infinitely often,
which contributes only finitely much to any particular partial sum, and that is
why Q3 does not follow from Q2.
