Erdős #16: the false step is now a proved refutation, not a sorry

The file already carried a careful note saying its monotonicity step is false and
that no bound will close it. The step was still written as a theorem with a
`sorry` under it, which is the one shape that cannot stand: a `sorry` on a false
statement is a live falsehood, and anything that ever discharged it would make
the file inconsistent. The claim is now the negation, and the negation is
proved.

The mechanism is one line of algebra. a_n < a_{n+1} exactly when p_n > n·g_n,
with g_n the gap — clear denominators and the comparison becomes
(n+1)p_n > n·p_{n+1}. That is rise_iff, and it needs no analysis at all. A small
gap therefore forces a rise as soon as p_n passes n·B, and p_n/n → ∞ puts every
fixed B under that line eventually. So bounded gaps plus super-linear growth
give not_eventually_monotone: a_n rises past every N, and the alternating series
test has no starting point. Both inputs are stated as Props and cited —
Zhang-Maynard for the gaps, the prime number theorem for the growth — rather
than assumed silently.

The twin primes make it concrete: a gap of 2 at index n raises the term whenever
p_n > 2n. The smallest instance is n = 12, where the primes are 41 and 43 and
12/41 < 13/43.

What survives is a_n → 0, one of Leibniz's two hypotheses and the easy one. The
convergence question itself is Erdős's and is open; it is a Prop here. In its
place the file had a `sorry`ed Cauchy argument whose first step already cited
the criterion it was supposed to establish.

The file also never compiled — it imported Mathlib.Data.Nat.Prime, which does
not exist in this toolchain — and was absent from the lakefile. It is in the
build now. Standard axioms.
