I formalized the rectangle-diagonal-into-torus picture, choosing what was actually provable out of a longer exposition rather than transcribing all of it. The exact, checkable core: a 16-by-3 rectangle glued into a torus has a corner-to-corner diagonal that is one closed curve touching every one of the 48 cells exactly once, because gcd(16,3)=1. Sixteen is SIXTEEN_3's own carrier, the sixteen subsets of {T,F,t,f}; three is its own three orderings, information, truth, constructivity. Forty-eight is the same Shavian 48 already held as twelve marks times four base logic values. This file is the other factorization of that same number, made a torus and checked as a real theorem.

New file: Imscribing/Paraconsistent/SixteenThreeTorus.lean. sixteen_three_coprime and torus_card ground the two numbers. diagonal is ZMod.chineseRemainder cited directly rather than reproved by hand, since Mathlib already carries the exact isomorphism ZMod 48 to ZMod 16 times ZMod 3 that this picture needs. diagonal_bijective is the main theorem: one walk of 48 steps, one visit to each cell, no repeats. every_corner_on_the_one_orbit states the corner-independence claim at the strength I could actually prove cleanly, surjectivity, every starting point sits somewhere on the one orbit, rather than reaching for a stronger bijectivity-of-the-shifted-map statement that would have needed hunting an uncertain Mathlib lemma name under time pressure.

What I left out on purpose: the embedded torus-knot trigonometric parametrization and the horn-torus pinch condition from the fuller exposition this was drawn from. Those are geometric illustrations, not exact claims tied to SIXTEEN_3's own structure, and formalizing "R plus r cos(3t) equals zero when cos(3t) equals negative one and R equals r" adds no real content, it is generic trigebra with nothing SIXTEEN_3-specific in it.
