Squarefree plus a power of two is false, and false infinitely often

The corpus asserted that every N ≥ 2 is a squarefree number plus a power of two.
It is not. 100 is the least counterexample — 99 = 9·11, 98 = 2·7², and 96, 92,
84, 68, 36 are all divisible by 4 — and the reason generalises into a congruence.

If 4 divides N then every k ≥ 2 leaves N − 2^k divisible by 4, so only N − 1 and
N − 2 can be squarefree. Force those to be multiples of 9 and 49 and nothing is
left: N ≡ 0 (mod 4), N ≡ 1 (mod 9), N ≡ 2 (mod 49), which is N ≡ 100 (mod 1764).
Every member of that family is a counterexample, the family is injective, so the
counterexamples are infinite. Erdos/SquarefreePlusPowerOfTwo.lean carries all of
it, and ErdosFormalize.SquarefreePlusPowerOfTwo is refuted by name.

What actually remains of Erdős's question is the density — how many integers
fail — and that is a Prop in the file, not a claim. The family already puts
x/1764 of them below x, which is the bound any density statement has to beat.
