Collatz: the conductor-three imbalance identity, proved, and the levels are sub-random

The predecessor face. I read the 3-adic profile off the real tree to depth 46 rather
than building an operator that would report its own construction, and the deviation
from uniform falls at every conductor -- 3, 9, 27, 81 -- at a fitted rate near 0.866.

That rate is also exactly (4/3)^(-1/2), the decay of pure sampling noise, since the
level grows by 4/3 per step. So I tested it against the noise floor of a uniform
random set of the same size. The measured deviation is BELOW that floor, and strongly
so at low conductor: mod 3 runs at 0.10 to 0.40 of it while mod 81 sits at 0.7 to 1.0,
at the floor. The levels are not merely equidistributed mod 3, they are more uniform
than random. The equidistribution is enforced, not statistical.

The mechanism is the involution, and the identity that carries it was only checked in
exact integers to depth 30. Now proved:

  imbalance_recursion   I_{d+1} = -I_d + (m2 - m8), over predStep, no exception
  cnt_predStep_one/two  each class of the next level, exactly: the swapped class
                        doubled, plus one class mod 9
  cnt_double_arm        the doubling arm's contribution
  cnt_odd_arm           the odd arm's, fixed by the parent mod 9
  predStep_disjoint     the arms are disjoint by parity
  oddArm_injOn          the odd arm is injective on junctions
  imbalance_two_step    two levels return the imbalance to itself up to the two
                        perturbations, so it is an ALTERNATING sum

The level-2 discrepancy in the old census was the script cutting the 1 -> 2 -> 1 edge,
which predStep does not do: checked again against the uncut tree, zero mismatches over
35 levels.

Measured alongside: |I_d| / sqrt(N) stays inside [0, 0.31] for d in 20..46, bounded
well below the random scale. The fitted exponent 0.23 covers a range where the sign
flips and is not a rate.
