Two Erdős statements given their real content, and proved

I ran the ninety nullary open Props in the ErdosFormalize batches past the
kernel with a single tactic line each. Seventy-five of them close: `trivial`
takes sixty-one. A statement that closes by `trivial` is not an open problem, it
is an empty statement wearing the name of one — `B3DensityUpper` said
`∃ C > 0, ∀ N, 0 ≤ C·√N`, which is true of C = 1 and never mentions a Sidon set.
Proving those as they stand would certify the defect, so they are not proved
here; they are being restated.

Two are done, with the mathematics constructed rather than asserted.

Erdos/SidonCounting.lean is the Erdős–Turán counting bound: on a Sidon set the
difference map is injective over the ordered pairs of distinct elements, so
k² − k ≤ 2N − 2 inside [1,N], and the density corollary follows without a square
root. The differences are taken over ℤ — on ℕ they truncate, which is how a bound
of this shape goes vacuous. The bound also has objects: the powers of four are a
Sidon set at every size, because a sum of two of them cannot reach the next
power of four, so the larger exponent is forced and cancelling it forces the
smaller.

Erdos/RatioSetSharp.lean proves RatioSetSharp: for every n there are n integers
with all n² − n + 1 ratios distinct. The witness attached to that statement was
geometric progressions, and it is wrong — a progression's ratios are r^(i−j) over
an arithmetic set of exponents, which collide down to 2n − 1, and that is a
theorem in the file now too. Sharpness needs exponents whose differences are all
distinct, which is a Sidon set, so the witness is 2 raised to the powers of four.

Both rest on propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound, nothing else. Library
green at 8592 jobs.
