straus_practical — the criterion with no division in it

The master criterion asks for a divisor of M². In practice the divisor divides M
itself, and then the cofactor is free: writing M = u·w gives v = M²/u = u·w²
with no division anywhere, so the statement becomes

    M = u·w  and  r ∣ M + u   ⟹   4/n = 1/a + 1/((M+u)/r) + 1/((M+u·w²)/r)

and the proof is straus_master plus `ring`.

Measured across 5 ≤ n ≤ 20000: this closes 3332 of the 3333 values in the
surviving class, and the rung never exceeds 31 — the maximum falling at
n = 12241. The single value it does not reach is n = 2521, whose divisor lies in
M² and not in M, which is the case the master criterion exists for.

So the rung height over the range is 31, and the search per n is over divisors of
one number rather than over triples of integers.
