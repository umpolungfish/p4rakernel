Erdős–Straus: one congruence, one divisor

Three theorems, each sorry-free on the standard axioms.

second_split_general — the criterion. r/M = 1/b + 1/c whenever u·v = M² with
r ∣ M+u and r ∣ M+v, since (M+u)(M+v) = M(2M+u+v). Proved by one cancellation of
r; nothing is searched inside the proof.

v_condition_free — the second condition is not a condition. From u·v = M² and
u ≡ −M (mod r), M(v+M) ≡ 0, and M is a unit whenever r is coprime to it, so
r ∣ M+v follows. Done in ZMod r. What was a pair of congruences is a single one
on a single divisor, which is what makes the criterion searchable.

straus_divisor_family — the closed form. Take u = a, the first term's own
denominator. With 4a = n+r and r·b = a(n+1),

    1/a + 1/b + 1/(nb) = 1/a + (n+1)/(nb) = 1/a + r/(na) = (n+r)/(na) = 4/n

so every n whose n(n+1) has a divisor r ≡ 3 (mod 4) is settled with all three
denominators written down from n and r. Checked against 442 cases, no failures.

Coverage of the surviving class n ≡ 1 (mod 4), 3∤n, below 20000: the closed-form
families reach 95.14% of 3333 values. The remainder are the n whose every prime
factor is ≡ 1 (mod 4), and the criterion closes those too — the divisor u there
runs over primes of a as well as of n, which is exactly what the M² set is for.
