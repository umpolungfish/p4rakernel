Triangle partition: the classification, and the 6 that separates two questions

The file said "Result: ALL n ≥ 1", attributed to Laczkovich 1990. That is a
conflation of three different questions, and the answer to the one the file
types is not all of ℕ.

A triangle is rep-n — dissected into n congruent pieces each SIMILAR to the
original — exactly when n is a square, a sum of two positive squares, or three
times a square (Snover–Waiveris–Williams 1991). 6 is none of those, and it is
the least excluded value: not_repArith_six and six_is_least_excluded prove both
halves by bounded search.

What makes the arithmetic appear at all is the area identity. n pieces of ratio
r exhaust the original, so n r² = 1 and r = 1/√n — rep_ratio_sq and rep_ratio.
The ratio is rational exactly for square n, which is why homothety subdivision
works there and why every other n needs a special triangle.

And 6 is exactly the witness that the two readings differ. Drop the similarity
requirement and 6 is available: the equilateral triangle's three altitudes cut
it into six congruent 30-60-90 triangles. That is machine-checked by SSS on
squared side lengths, so no square root is extracted — every piece has squared
sides 1/4, 1/12, 1/3, and piece_not_similar_to_original shows none of them is
similar to the equilateral original, whose squared sides are all 1. Laczkovich's
theorem is about tilings by similar triangles of possibly different sizes, a
third question again.
