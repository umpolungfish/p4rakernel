Ramsey asymptotics: the Stirling route never closed, and the refined one does

§7 paired R_k(C_{2n+1}) ≤ C_n^k·(k!)^{1/n} with R_k(K_3) ≥ (2.52)^k and concluded
the limit is 0. Those two do not give it. (k!)^{1/n} is exp(k log k / n) to
leading order, which outgrows every exponential c^k — the file called it
"subexponential in k", the opposite of what it is. Against a merely exponential
lower bound that ratio tends to infinity. stirling_beats_exponential proves the
comparison at the level of exponents: log k > n log c as soon as k > c^n, so no
exponential lower bound can dominate. Branch 3 reaches 0 only under the
conjecture R_k(K_3) ∼ k!, which the same docstring lists as open two entries
later.

§15's pairing is the one that works, and it is now proved rather than described.
Bondy-Erdős bounds the numerator by (2n+1)·2^k and Schur bounds the denominator
below by 3.199^k, so the ratio is at most (2n+1)·(2/3.199)^k — refined_ratio_bound
— and that vanishes by geometric decay alone, refined_ratio_tendsto_zero. No
factorial asymptotics anywhere, and it holds for every fixed n ≥ 1 rather than
only n ≥ 2. ramsey_base_comparison_note was `: True`; it is now
ramsey_base_comparison carrying both halves.
