The representation function of a Sidon set is bounded by two.

An external theorem, not internal to the Grammar, on the conjecture the tools
were pointed at.

The Erdos-Turan conjecture on additive bases says an asymptotic basis of order
two has unbounded representation function r(n) = #{(a,b) : a+b = n}. It is open.
This proves the bridge between that conjecture and the Sidon condition, which is
where the search for a counterexample lives.

sidon_reps_le_two. For every n, with no hypothesis on n and no asymptotics, a
Sidon set admits at most two ordered representations. The Sidon property fixes
the unordered pair and the ordered pairs over it are that pair and its swap. The
constant is right rather than merely an upper bound: sidon_reps_eq_two_example
attains it.

sidon_not_basis_of_erdos_turan. If the conjecture holds, no set Sidon on every
finite window is an asymptotic basis of order two. The conjecture demands counts
above every bound and the theorem caps them at two. Which is why Sidon sets are
where a counterexample would have to be, and why none has come from there: the
property making the sums nearly injective is the property bounding r.

The route came from the tools rather than from me. shared_floor put the proven
attractor at the tuple with the criticality slot at the complex-plane value
while the proved counting structure sits at criticality, so the gap it named was
the move from a critical-exponent count to complex analysis. That is the actual
route in this area, elementary counting to generating functions on the circle,
and the bridge such a route needs is the bound on r, which the repo did not
have. The proof shape is the banked count: fix n, hold the pair in scope while
the swap runs, fuse. The swap is the same involution that carries
card_unorderedPairs in ErdosTuranBound.
