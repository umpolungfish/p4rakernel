UNSOLVED: Euclidean geometry, and planarity built

Falconer through a distance set, Danzer sets as meeting every unit-volume convex
body, Thomson's energy as the Coulomb sum with its asymptotic constant, and knot
thickness for ropelength. The source's dead-fly entry asked for bounded
separation, which a lattice has and which is not the obstruction; it asks for
bounded density now.

Planarity is the one the source kept naming and never had. A drawing places
vertices in the plane with straight edges, a crossing is two independent edges
whose open segments meet, and planar means some drawing has none. Harborth then
says what it should — a crossing-free integer-length drawing — and Barnette,
which I had skipped for want of planarity, is stated: three-connected cubic
bipartite planar graphs have a Hamiltonian cycle, with three-connectivity as
survival of any two deletions.
