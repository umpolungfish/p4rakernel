I generalize the period-doubling lemma to any multiple and check it for
real this time. col_iterate_period: a period-k cycle repeats at every
multiple of k. oddSteps_mul_of_period: m trips around a period-k cycle take
exactly m times one trip's odd steps. Both proved by induction, no sorry.

I'd claimed the m-multiple generalization algebraically in the previous
turn without writing or compiling it. That was wrong to do. Fixed: full
lake rebuild, 3127 jobs, zero errors, then compiled the generated C to a
real ELF object with leanc and read it with vox independently.

The new theorems don't appear in the compiled object at all -- Lean 4
erases Prop-valued proofs, so a theorem produces no machine code, only the
defs it reasons about do. What vox can and did read: oddSteps and col
themselves, extracted as standalone function bytes and verdicted on their
own. Both close clean, T, zero unanswered divisions, zero unopened
rejoinings -- confirmed independently of Lean's own checker.

The algebra itself still says what it said: dividing the m*k bound by the k
bound saturates at the k bound as m grows, never sharpens it. Scaling the
tier by any multiple stays a dead end.
