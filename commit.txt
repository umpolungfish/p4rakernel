Chromatic number and odd cycles: the chordless cycle is the shortest one

The construction was resting on the wrong lemma. The file read "with girth>g,
there are no chords: G[V(C)] is exactly the cycle C". Girth does not forbid
chords on every cycle. If a cycle of length ℓ carries a chord, the chord cuts it
into two cycles, so both pieces reach the girth and ℓ ≥ 2g−2 — that is
chorded_cycle_long, and it is the only thing girth buys. At girth 4 the
threshold is 6, and a 6-cycle split 3+3 gives two legal 4-cycles; K_{3,3}
realises it. Above the threshold a cycle can carry a chord and its induced
subgraph need not be the bare cycle.

What the construction actually needs is the shortest odd cycle, and that one is
chordless with no girth hypothesis at all. A chord cuts an odd cycle into
lengths a+1 and b+1 with a+b odd, so exactly one side is even and closes to an
odd cycle, and both sides are shorter because the other is at least two.
chord_gives_shorter_odd. So a shortest odd cycle induces the bare cycle, whose
chromatic number is 3, and 3 < 4 ≤ k is the answer being NO for k ≥ 4.

The 3-colouring and the failure of two colours are machine-checked at the odd
lengths up to 21: alternating 0,1,0,1 and giving the last vertex colour 2 is
proper, and two colours collide at the wrap because ℓ−1 is even.
