Prove what is not enough, so the k ≥ 4 target is an inequality and not a mood

The reduction is worth nothing until a bound exists for k ≥ 4, and "the known
bounds fall short" was a remark. It is two theorems now.

not_summable_one_div_log_rpow: for every c > 0, Σ 1/(log j)^c diverges. Gowers'
bound for general k, and the Leng–Sah–Sawhney improvement, have the shape
r_k(N) ≤ C·N/(log log N)^c or weaker; at N = 2^j that leaves terms of order
1/(log j)^c. So no constant and no exponent c rescues that shape — it is
insufficient as a shape, not merely insufficiently strong. Proved by log j ≤
c·j^(1/c), so (log j)^c ≤ c^c·j, and comparison with the harmonic series.

not_summable_at_exponent_one: a bound C·N/log N gives dyadic terms C/(j log 2),
which is harmonic. That is the knife-edge, and it is where Roth's own bound sits
at k = 3.

Between them the target is exact. What is needed is polylogarithmic in N, past
exponent one. At k = 3 that is Bloom–Sisask. At k = 4 a bound of the right SHAPE
exists — Green–Tao's r_4(N) ≪ N/(log N)^c — with an exponent far below one, so
the door there is not "prove something polylogarithmic" but "push a known
exponent past 1". For k ≥ 5 the shape itself is not yet reached.

No axioms; propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound throughout.
