Erdős–Straus: the greedy family covers most of the surviving class

I read the conjecture through the kernel's own instrument before touching it.
`nesting greedy` puts it in the ATTRACTED bin with a finite budget — greedy
unit-fraction removal always arrives, so Erdős–Straus is a claim about the
BUDGET being three, not about arrival. That is a different question from the one
the "never" machinery answers, and knowing which one it is came from the
instrument, not from me. I added the greedy map to src/nesting.rs so the reading
is reproducible.

The one-shot shape then gives the mechanism: #1 reads a period off the
denominator structure rather than iterating, and the greedy step does the same
here. For n = 4k+1 the largest unit fraction below 4/n is 1/(k+1), and

    4/(4k+1) − 1/(k+1) = 3/((4k+1)(k+1))

with numerator exactly 3, always. So the surviving class collapses onto one
question — when is 3/m a sum of two unit fractions — and that has a divisor
answer: for m = (3t+2)·e,

    3/m = 1/(e(t+1)) + 1/(m(t+1))

Composing them gives an explicit three-term representation whose denominators
are written down from the factorisation, none of them searched for.

Erdos/StrausGreedyFamily.lean carries both identities and the composition,
sorry-free on the standard axioms. What the family does not reach is m whose
prime factors are all ≡ 1 (mod 3); across n < 100000 in the class that is 6.8%,
the family closing the other 93.2%. The residue is a Prop, named GreedyResidue,
because that is where the difficulty now sits and nothing here settles it.
