The axiom was false, and the lift could not see it

Problem0003 answered Erdős 3 at k=3 from an axiom asserting that divergent
reciprocals give positive lower density. That is false, and my own corpus holds
the counterexample: ErdosTuran.primes_reciprocal_diverges proves Σ1/p diverges,
and π(N)/N → 0, so no ε survives every N. Everything below it proved nothing.

It was also the whole problem. Erdős 3 is interesting BECAUSE divergence does not
give density — that gap is why the primes needed Green–Tao. Assuming it away
assumed away the question.

Split by what it costs. three_ap_of_dense is the half Roth settles, and it is now
proved: one window wide enough for the corners bound, no uniform density over
every N, nothing assumed beyond Lean's foundation. The other half is
Bloom–Sisask 2020, a real theorem that is not in Mathlib, so it stands as an
axiom named for the paper instead of dressed as a density claim.

The lift was blind to all of it. isSorry matched sorryAx alone, so a declared
axiom was an ordinary constant and lifted to ◻, a commit, reading exactly like a
proved lemma. Every theorem resting on the false axiom verdicted T or N. An axiom
opens what nothing closes, so it now lifts where a sorry lifts, and transitively,
because a theorem whose body is one citation of an assumption is resting on that
assumption. The transitive walk is scoped to names this corpus declares: a
Mathlib constant was elaborated before this corpus existed and cannot rest on its
axioms, and collecting axioms for every Mathlib name in every term does not
finish — that run went ten minutes before I killed it. Scoped, it takes twelve
seconds.

Problem0041 stated its bound as ncard / (N^(1/3) : ℝ), where 1/3 is ℕ division,
so the exponent was 0, the denominator was 1, and the goal read |A ∩ [1,N]| → 0 —
false for every infinite A, and reachable only through the sorry. The exponent is
real now. The counting bound is proved: the sum map is injective on 3-subsets by
hypothesis and every sum lands in [3,3N], so C(k,3) ≤ 3N. The o(N^(1/3)) is the
open question and stays open. The file was also an orphan, in no glob and
unreachable by lake build; it is in the tree now.

Verdicts track the axiom ledger exactly: T where #print axioms shows the
foundational three, B where it shows more.
