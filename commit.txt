Problem 3 needs no axiom of its own — the corpus already assumes enough

I had put a Bloom–Sisask axiom in Problem0003, at the conclusion: divergent
reciprocals give a progression. Then I went looking for the paper to cite
properly and found the corpus had already answered. Imscribing.Classical.Szemeredi
carries kelley_meka_upper_bound — r₃(N) ≤ N·exp(−c·(log N)^(1/11)) — which is
quantitative, strictly stronger than the Bloom–Sisask bound that first crossed
this threshold, and more than enough to give what I was assuming. My axiom was
one assumption stated twice, the weaker time.

The same file also already axiomatizes gowers_norm_lower_bound, inverse_theorem
and density_increment. The grammar sweep had named gowers_u3_norm as the only
reagent that both clicks with the barrier and supplies the missing merge; that
machinery was in the corpus before the sweep ran, in a different file.

So the axiom is gone, and nothing replaced it. The remaining step —
ReciprocalSumConverges, that a progression-free set has convergent reciprocal sum
— is a HYPOTHESIS now, threaded through the three theorems that need it. That is
neither an assertion nor a hole: the debt sits in the type where a reader sees it
without running #print axioms, and anyone who discharges it gets the theorem
unconditionally.

Problem0003 now declares no axioms and carries no sorry. three_ap_of_dense,
three_ap_of_not_threeAPFree, block_card_le and erdos_problem_3_k3 all rest on
propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound alone.

The lift moved with it: erdos_problem_3_k3 read B while it stood on an axiom and
reads T now. Not bookkeeping — with the hypothesis as an argument the proof term
has no unpaired fork, so the word closes. What was owed did not disappear, it
went somewhere it can be seen.

Also names both papers properly: Kelley–Meka arXiv:2302.05537, Bloom–Sisask
arXiv:2007.03528. The switch reads them as a toggle, Kelley–Meka the revealed
form and Bloom–Sisask the hidden one, with ∋ among the coupled consequences.
