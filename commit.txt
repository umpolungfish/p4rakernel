Erdos-Straus: the price-zero layer, and the frontier it leaves

The one-shot at n = 5 (mod 8) was not the only rung read off n. Writing the
divisor as u = n^i a^j and using 4a = n (mod r), the congruence r | M+u leaves
exactly three live outcomes: r | n, r | n+1, r | n+4. Every other exponent pair
demands r | 8, r | 5 or r | n^2+4, all dead for r = 3 (mod 4).

So I collected the three families into one theorem, straus_of_priceZero, which
takes the divisibility and hands back the three denominators with nothing
searched. Added multiplicative descent, and the frontier: what the price-zero
layer and the one-shot both miss. Every frontier value is n = 1 (mod 24), and
that is proved rather than observed.

Measured over 5 <= n <= 200000: 32062 of the 33333 values in the surviving class
close at price zero. 1271 remain, and descent through a proper divisor takes 639
of those, leaving 632.
