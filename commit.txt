Erdős-Kac interval: the interval has no prime powers, and T with N is T

A constraint the decomposition never states, and it is immediate. A prime power
has ω(n)=1, and log log n passes 1 at e^e ≈ 15.154, so past 16 no prime power
can satisfy ω(n) > log log n. An interval on which the inequality holds
EVERYWHERE is therefore free of prime powers, and its length is capped by the
largest prime-power gap in [x,2x] — which is where the difficulty actually
sits. loglog_sixteen_gt_one pins the threshold from log 16 = 4 log 2 > 2.7725
against e < 2.7183, and prime_power_excluded and interval_avoids_primes carry it
to every n ≥ 16.

The verdict was B on the wrong pair. It read "Both True (CRT bound is
unconditional) and Neither (Erdős's conjecture is completely open)". B is
Both-True-and-False; True with Neither is not it. The kernel's own ffuse returns
B only from (T,F) and (F,T) and is the Belnap join everywhere else, which is
T-dominant, so the pair described fuses to T. ffuse_true_neither is that fuse,
checked against BelnapSplitFuse rather than asserted.

And the gap was measured one logarithm out. With L = log x the CRT bound is
L/(log L)² and the conjecture is L^k, so the ratio is L^(k−1)(log L)² —
polynomial in L, exponential in log L. Exponential in log x would be e^(cL) =
x^c, the size of the whole interval [x,2x] rather than a gap between two bounds
inside it.
