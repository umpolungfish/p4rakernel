A scan for counts carried in the open through a reversal.

The IMASM reading is that AREV empties the register and leaves open frames
alone, so a result fused back to depth zero is exposed to the next reversal
while the same result held one level up survives it.

Lean banks automatically. A `have` stays in the local context for the rest of
the declaration and nothing can clear it, so the literal failure cannot occur
here. What can is the arrangement it corresponds to: a cardinality computed
inline in a `calc` that also reverses direction, with no prior `have` holding
it, so every step after the turn re-derives the count.

The corpus is clean by this test. Fifty files carry a calc, seven chains carry
a cardinality, and every one of those that reverses is drawing on a banked fact.

Two false positives were removed rather than tolerated, since a lint that cries
is worse than no lint. `have h := f args` states its content in h's elaborated
type and not in its syntax, so looking for the carried term textually inside the
have misses it entirely; a chain using h is drawing on a banked fact whatever
h's type turns out to say, and deciding more than that needs the elaborator. And
a `.symm` inside a simp argument list is a side condition rather than a turn in
the chain, so bracketed arguments are stripped before looking.

Reads only. Elaborates nothing, changes nothing, so a hit is a candidate to look
at rather than a defect.
