Pulled the three papers, and one of my claims was wrong

Checked the literature instead of citing it from memory, and found I had
conflated two different shapes.

Bloom–Sisask (arXiv:2007.03528), abstract verbatim: |A| ≪ N/(log N)^{1+c} for
absolute c > 0. Exactly the shape erdos_conjecture_of_bound consumes, past
exponent one. k = 3 closes.

Green–Tao III (arXiv:1705.01703): r_4(N) ≪ N(log N)^{-c}, absolute c > 0, "the
limit of our methods". Their own notational convention records 0 < c ≪ 1. Right
shape, exponent below one.

Leng–Sah–Sawhney (arXiv:2402.17995), Theorem 1.1 verbatim: "Fix k ≥ 5. There is
c_k ∈ (0,1) such that r_k(N) ≪ N exp(-(log log N)^{c_k})." The interval is
theirs.

The error: I had written that a log-log bound is insufficient at any exponent and
attached Leng–Sah–Sawhney to that claim. The theorem I proved is about
1/(log j)^c, which is what Gowers' N/(log log N)^c gives. Leng–Sah–Sawhney give
exp(-(log j)^{c_k}) at the dyadic scale, which is a different shape, and it is
NOT insufficient at any exponent — it converges precisely when c_k > 1.

So two more theorems, and they change what the open cases are.
not_summable_exp_neg_log_rpow: the shape diverges for c ≤ 1.
summable_exp_neg_log_rpow: it converges for c > 1.

Which makes both open cases the same kind of thing. At k = 4 a polylogarithmic
exponent must cross one; at k ≥ 5 a log-log exponent must. Each is a single
number in a published theorem, and in each the authors state their number is
below the threshold. Not a programme — a constant.
