The numerator three was never special — Erdős–Straus has a ladder

I took the residue back to the instruments rather than to a search. `imasm check`
on the two-fork form returns B (open): "a δ fork dangles; fuse them, or COMMIT
one arm (IFIX)". Committing the arm means the second choice comes from a rule,
and the rule is that the greedy step is only the first rung.

For n = 4k+1 and any r ≡ 3 (mod 4), the term a = (n+r)/4 is an integer k+j+1 and

    4/n − 1/a = r/(n·a)

so the remainder's numerator is CHOSEN, not met. And the two-term identity never
needed r = 3 either: for M = d·e with d ≡ −1 (mod r) and d = r·t + (r−1),

    r/M = 1/(e(t+1)) + 1/(M(t+1))

which holds for every r ≥ 2. At r = 1 the divisor can vanish, and a numerator of
one is already a unit fraction, so two is the honest bound.

The greedy family proved earlier is the r = 3 rung. Each further rung is another
covering condition on the same n, and what one rung misses another can catch:
n = 49 — the first value the greedy family leaves — closes on the r = 7 rung in
TWO terms, 4/49 = 1/14 + 1/98.

All of it sorry-free on the standard axioms, library green at 8592 jobs. The
conjecture is not closed: the ladder gives infinitely many covering conditions
but no proof that some rung always catches. That is now the precise question,
and it is a question about the rungs rather than about n.
