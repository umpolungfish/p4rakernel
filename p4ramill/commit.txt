Perfect cuboid: consolidate three descent axioms into one named residual

A Grammar-first workflow, decomposed to Lean. The descent protocol imscribes and
closes as a shape (imasm check ⊢∈⊤⊥∋◻⊣ → T), but the kernel closure verdict on
the assembled theorem is OPEN — it rested on the opaque axiom
descent_operator_exists. The catalog locates the cut on the ⊥ (chirality) axis:
the descent imscriptions split 𐑖 (two-step, bounded — cases A and B) versus 𐑫
(inexhaustible — case C), and that promotion is the one unclosed step.

CaseC.lean decomposes it into a complete case split and discharges what is
already proven:
  A. diagonal even  → all seven entries even (all_seven_even_of_g_even, new,
     from the mod-4 face equations) → halve to a strictly smaller cuboid
     (halve_g_smaller, proven).
  B. edge-pairs coprime → impossible (case_all_gcd_one_impossible, proven).
  C. diagonal odd, some pair shares a factor → one axiom,
     case_mixed_parity_impossible, stating exactly the open mixed-parity content.

descent_or_impossible and no_perfect_cuboid are theorems from these. The kernel
confirms the result:
  #print axioms no_perfect_cuboid
  → [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound, case_mixed_parity_impossible]

Three opaque descent axioms become one precisely-named residual; cases A and B
are proof, not assumption. The conjecture is not closed — the residual axiom IS
the conjecture on its branch — and that is stated plainly. The workflow is
written up in ig-docs/perfect_cuboid_grammar_workflow.md as the reference
example for the specialists.
